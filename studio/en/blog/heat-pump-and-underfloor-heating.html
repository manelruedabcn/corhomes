ENERGY AND COMFORT

Air-to-water heat pumps and underfloor heating in a renovation

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Air-to-water heat pumps have become the usual choice when a home's heating and cooling is renewed. Combined with underfloor heating they offer comfort that is very hard to match, but it isn't always possible or necessary. The key is to decide according to the scope of the renovation.

What an air-to-water heat pump is

It is a heat pump that extracts energy from the outside air and transfers it to the water in the home's system. It can provide heating in winter, cooling in summer and domestic hot water. For every unit of electricity it uses, it delivers several units of heat, which is why it is so efficient.

Why it pairs so well with underfloor heating

A heat pump is more efficient the lower the temperature it has to heat water to. Underfloor heating works with low-temperature water, roughly 30 to 45 °C, while a conventional radiator needs considerably more. That is why a heat pump with underfloor heating is the most efficient and comfortable combination.

The comfort is different, too: heat rises evenly from the floor, there are no draughts, no radiators taking up walls and the temperature is very stable.

When it makes sense in a renovation

Installing underfloor heating means lifting the floor and laying insulation, pipes and a new screed. So it fits:

  • Full renovations where all the floors are being replaced.
  • Ground floors or houses where the floor is being redone.
  • Homes that can accommodate a floor several centimetres higher.

If the renovation doesn't touch the floors, there are other options.

Alternatives to underfloor heating

  • Low-temperature radiators: larger than conventional ones, they work with cooler water and make good use of the heat pump.
  • Fan coils: units that blow warm or cool air using water from the heat pump. They serve for both heating and cooling and need little building work.
  • High-temperature heat pumps: let you keep existing radiators, although with lower efficiency.
  • Air-to-air heat pumps (splits or ducted): they don't provide hot water, but they are a simple way to heat and cool.

And in summer?

The same underfloor system can cool by circulating colder water, around 14 to 18 °C. The cooling is gentle and silent, but it has a limit: it doesn't dehumidify. In humid coastal areas, such as much of Garraf, it should be combined with dew-point control and a system that removes moisture, such as fan coils or ventilation with air treatment. We explain it in cooling a Mediterranean home.

What to plan for on site

  • Where the outdoor unit goes: it needs airflow, access for maintenance and must not disturb neighbours with noise. In apartment buildings, check the community rules on façades and roofs.
  • Space for the hot water tank and the indoor hydraulic module.
  • Insulation under the underfloor heating, especially on the ground floor.
  • Zoning: thermostats per room or per zone so you don't heat what isn't used.
  • Floor height: check how it affects doors and junctions with other rooms.
  • Electrical installation: enough contracted power.

First, reduce demand

Before sizing the equipment, it is worth improving the building envelope: insulation, windows and airtightness. A house that loses less energy needs a smaller, cheaper unit that uses less. That is the logic of the near-passive house.

Maintenance

A heat pump needs periodic servicing, filter cleaning and circuit checks. It is simple maintenance, but worth contracting so the unit performs properly for years.

Grants

From time to time there are public grants for energy renovation and heat pump installation. They change often, so check them when planning the work.
