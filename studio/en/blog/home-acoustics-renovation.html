Home acoustics: how to reduce noise when you renovate

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Noise is one of the hardest nuisances to put up with at home, and also one of the best solved if it is considered during the renovation. To do it well you need to know where it comes from, because each type of noise has its own solution.

Three types of noise

  • Outdoor airborne noise: traffic, bar terraces, building work. It gets in mainly through windows and shutter boxes.
  • Airborne noise between homes or rooms: voices, television, music. It passes through partitions and floors.
  • Impact noise: footsteps, furniture being dragged, things falling. It travels through the structure, especially to the flat below.

Windows: the first line of defence

If the noise comes from the street, windows are the key. Acoustic laminated glass, with an interlayer that dampens vibration, and an asymmetric build-up, with panes of different thickness, greatly improve insulation. The airtightness of the window and the shutter box matters just as much: noise gets in through a gap even if the glass is excellent. We go into detail in energy-efficient windows.

Partitions and party walls

To improve insulation between rooms or with the neighbouring home, the usual approach is a lining wall: a second leaf separated from the existing wall, with absorbent material such as mineral wool in the cavity. The key is the mass-spring-mass principle: two heavy layers separated by a material that absorbs.

Details that make a difference:

  • Don't fix the new leaf rigidly to the existing wall.
  • Seal the perimeter and the junctions with ceiling and floor.
  • Avoid back-to-back socket boxes on both sides of the partition.

Floors and impact noise

An acoustic underlay under the flooring, or a floating floor on a resilient layer, greatly reduces the noise reaching the home below. If underfloor heating is installed, the insulation under the pipes helps too. Rugs also absorb footsteps.

Ceilings

If noise comes from above, a suspended ceiling on acoustic hangers with mineral wool in the cavity reduces transmission. It is an effective solution in bedrooms.

Quiet services

  • Soundproofed or insulated drain stacks, so you don't hear water passing.
  • Quiet heating, cooling and ventilation units, with anti-vibration mounts.
  • Solid doors with seals in bedrooms and offices.

Inside the room: reverberation

A room with hard surfaces and little furniture echoes, even if no outside noise gets in. It is corrected with absorbent elements: rugs, thick curtains, upholstered sofas, shelves of books, wooden or fabric acoustic panels. In open spaces with high ceilings it is especially important.

Priorities by problem

  1. Noisy street: acoustic windows and an airtight shutter box.
  2. Neighbours: lining walls on party walls and, if needed, a suspended ceiling.
  3. Disturbing the flat below: acoustic underlay or a floating floor.
  4. Echo inside: textiles, furniture and absorption.

A quieter home

Quiet is one of the pillars of wellbeing at home, and it is essential in a home office or a calm space.
