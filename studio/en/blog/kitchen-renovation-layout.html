Kitchen renovation: a layout that works

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

The kitchen is the most used room and the most expensive per square metre in a renovation. So it pays to get the layout right before choosing colours or materials. A beautiful kitchen that makes you take ten steps for everything ends up being a daily frustration.

Think about how you cook

Before drawing, answer: how many people cook at once? Do you eat in the kitchen? How much storage do you need? Do you cook every day or occasionally? Do you want it open to the living room? The answers define the layout.

The work zones

Kitchen work follows a natural order: storing, washing, preparing, cooking and serving. Arranging the kitchen in that sequence saves steps:

  1. Storage: pantry, fridge, tall units.
  2. Washing: sink and dishwasher, together.
  3. Preparation: the largest clear stretch of worktop, between sink and hob.
  4. Cooking: hob, oven, extractor hood.
  5. Serving: close to the table or the way into the dining area.

The work triangle

A classic reference is the triangle formed by fridge, sink and hob. The sum of its three sides should be roughly between 4 and 8 metres: less feels cramped, more means too much walking. And no household through-route should cross that triangle.

Layout types

  • Single-wall: everything on one wall. For narrow kitchens or small open ones.
  • Galley: two facing runs. Very efficient if there is at least 1.10 to 1.20 m between them.
  • L-shaped: uses a corner and leaves room for a table.
  • U-shaped: plenty of worktop and storage; needs some width.
  • With an island or peninsula: adds surface and a meeting point. An island needs enough clearance around it, about 1 m or more.

Smart storage

  • Drawers instead of doors in base units: you see everything at a glance.
  • Units up to the ceiling to use the full height.
  • Tall housings for oven, microwave and pantry.
  • Solutions for corners, which are usually wasted space.
  • A built-in place for recycling.

Worktops

Natural stone, compact and porcelain surfaces and engineered materials differ in resistance to stains, heat and scratches. Choose according to real use, not just looks. Stone or porcelain worktops are templated and made once the units are installed, and that lead time must be in the schedule.

Services before units

Water, waste, gas if any, sockets and the extractor outlet are positioned according to the final furniture design. That is why the kitchen is fully designed before work starts, not afterwards. We explain it in the order of trades.

Plan enough sockets above the worktop for small appliances, and an extractor that vents outside whenever possible.

Lighting

General light, task light under the wall units and a pendant over the table or island. Temperatures of 3000 to 3500 K and good colour rendering to see food as it is. More in layered lighting.

Open or closed kitchen

Opening the kitchen to the living room gains light and space, but it demands a good extractor, quiet appliances and tidiness. A middle way is a glazed screen or a sliding door that opens or closes depending on the moment.

The budget

In a kitchen renovation, units, worktop and appliances weigh heavily. Ask for an itemised quote that also includes building work, services and finishing details. We help you read it in itemised renovation quotes.
