LIGHT

How to gain natural light in a renovation

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

A home with good natural light feels larger, cleaner and more cheerful, and it is better to live in. Many homes have more light available than they use: it is blocked by partitions, solid doors or dark finishes. A renovation is the moment to set it free.

Start with orientation

Before changing anything, watch how light enters each room through the day and the year. At our latitude, south-facing rooms get direct sun in winter and can easily be shaded in summer with eaves or awnings. East-facing rooms get morning light, and west-facing ones get strong afternoon sun that is best filtered. Knowing this helps decide where to place the kitchen, the living room or the office.

Open up the layout

Many homes have their light trapped in a single room. Opening the kitchen to the living room, removing an enclosed corridor or joining two small rooms lets light reach further in. Before demolishing, always check which walls are structural and which permits the work needs.

Glazed partitions and doors

When you don't want to lose the separation, a glazed partition or a door with glass lets light through while keeping some independence. Translucent or frosted glass is useful for bathrooms and bedrooms. A glass fanlight above a door or partition is a discreet and very effective solution in hallways.

Enlarge or create openings

Making a window bigger, lowering the sill or turning a window into a door to the terrace can transform a room. This work affects the façade and often the structure, so it needs a technical project and a licence, and sometimes the owners' association's approval.

Take the opportunity to replace the frames with more efficient ones; we explain how in energy-efficient windows.

Skylights and light tubes

In top-floor flats or houses, a skylight or roof window brings overhead light to stairs, bathrooms or hallways. When the distance to the roof is large, solar tubes carry light through a reflective duct down to the room's ceiling.

Colours and finishes

  • Walls and ceilings in warm whites or very light tones reflect more light.
  • Matt or satin finishes spread it better than gloss, which creates glare.
  • Light floors, in pale wood, stone or microcement, increase brightness.
  • Lime adds a texture that softens and diffuses light.

Mirrors with intention

A mirror opposite or beside a window doubles the sense of light and depth. It works especially well in hallways and entrances.

Frames and shading

Slimmer profiles leave more glass area. And exterior solar protection, such as adjustable shutters, lattices or awnings, lets you have large windows without overheating the house in summer. It is better to stop the sun before it reaches the glass than to try to cool the room afterwards.

Fewer obstacles

Sometimes light is lost for simple reasons: heavy curtains, tall furniture in front of windows, blinds always half down. Rearranging furniture and choosing light curtains costs nothing and makes a difference.

And when evening falls

Natural light is complemented by good artificial lighting. In layered lighting we explain how to keep the warmth once the sun goes down.
