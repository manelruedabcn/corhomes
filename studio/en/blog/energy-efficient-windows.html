Energy-efficient windows: what to check before replacing them

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Windows are the weakest point of a home's envelope: heat escapes through them in winter, heat comes in through them in summer and street noise filters in. Replacing them is one of the improvements you notice most, as long as they are well chosen and well installed.

The frame

  • PVC: very good thermal insulation, low maintenance and a good price. Multi-chamber, reinforced profiles are the most efficient.
  • Thermally broken aluminium: slimmer, stronger profiles with an insulating barrier inside that reduces losses. Without a thermal break, aluminium insulates poorly.
  • Wood: good insulation and great warmth; it needs periodic maintenance.
  • Wood-aluminium: wood inside and aluminium outside, combining looks and durability.

The glass

It is the largest surface of the window, so choosing it is decisive:

  • Double glazing with a cavity at least, ideally argon-filled.
  • Low-emissivity glass: an almost invisible coating that greatly reduces heat loss.
  • Solar control: limits heat from the sun without darkening too much. Highly recommended for south and west orientations in our climate.
  • Acoustic laminated glass if the street is noisy. We discuss it in home acoustics.
  • Triple glazing: even more insulation; in a Mediterranean climate it makes sense in specific cases or in projects with passive criteria.

Thermal transmittance (U-value)

It shows how much heat passes through the window: the lower, the better it insulates. Look at the value for the complete window, Uw, not just the glass or the frame separately. Ask the manufacturer to state it in the quote.

Opening type

  • Casement and tilt-and-turn: they close against seals and are the most airtight.
  • Sliding: convenient and space-saving, but conventional ones are less airtight. Lift-and-slide systems, which raise the sash when closing, greatly improve airtightness.

The shutter box

A classic point of air leaks and cold. If you are replacing windows, replace or insulate the box, or consider alternatives such as shutters with an external box or hinged shutters.

Installation matters as much as the window

An excellent window badly installed performs like a mediocre one. Take care of:

  • Sealing between frame and wall with suitable tapes or foams, not just silicone.
  • Continuity with the façade insulation to avoid thermal bridges.
  • A sill and drip that drain water properly.

It is one of the stages that benefits most from project management, because once the trim is finished, defects can no longer be seen.

Solar shading

In summer, the best partner for a good window is exterior shading: adjustable shutters, awnings, lattices or eaves. Stopping the sun outside is far more effective than inside. We explain it in cooling a Mediterranean home.

Near the sea

On the coast, choose hardware and profiles with treatments resistant to the marine environment and finishes certified for aggressive atmospheres. We cover it in renovating near the sea.

Permits and the community

Replacing windows changes the look of the façade. In apartment buildings the community often sets the colour, material and glazing bars. Also check whether the town hall requires any procedure; there is guidance in renovation permits.

What to ask for in the quote

  1. Profile material and series.
  2. Glass composition.
  3. Uw value of the complete window.
  4. Opening type and hardware.
  5. Solution for the shutter box.
  6. Sealing system and trims included.
  7. Removal of the old windows.
