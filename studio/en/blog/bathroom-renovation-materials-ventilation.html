Bathroom renovation: materials, shower and ventilation

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

A well-renovated bathroom lasts for years without trouble. A badly done one gives warning soon: black grout, damp on the neighbour's ceiling or a tray that pools water. The difference is almost never in the tile you chose, but in what lies beneath it.

The flush shower

Replacing the bath with a level-access shower is one of the most requested renovations: more space, better accessibility and a cleaner look. Options:

  • Low-profile tray, sitting on the floor.
  • Flush tray, set level with the floor.
  • Wet-room shower with the same finish as the floor and a fall towards a linear drain.

The floor needs a fall towards the drain, usually 1 to 2 %, to avoid puddles. In flats, the height available for the waste pipe determines which solution is possible.

Waterproofing

It is the most important step and the one you don't see. In the shower area, floor and walls need a continuous waterproof membrane or mortar, reinforced at corners and at the junction with the drain. In wet-room showers it is essential. A mistake here can't be fixed without lifting everything.

Materials

  • Non-slip floor, especially in the shower. Look for tiles with a slip rating suitable for wet areas.
  • Porcelain: tough, low-porosity and easy to maintain.
  • Natural stone such as travertine or limestone: beautiful and warm, it needs a water-repellent treatment and some upkeep.
  • Microcement: continuous and jointless, it requires careful application and good sealing.
  • Large-format tiles on walls reduce the number of joints and make cleaning easier.
  • Epoxy grout in shower areas: more resistant to stains and moisture.

Ventilation

Humidity is the bathroom's great enemy. A window helps, but it isn't enough in winter. An extractor fan with a timer or humidity sensor prevents condensation and mould. If the home has heat recovery ventilation, the bathroom is one of the extract points.

Lighting

Front lighting at the mirror, at face height, to avoid shadows. General light suitable for wet areas and, if you like, a low indirect light for night-time. Temperatures of 3000 to 3500 K and good colour rendering. More in layered lighting.

Taps and sanitaryware

  • A thermostatic mixer in the shower to keep the temperature stable.
  • Wall-hung toilets: easier to clean, although they need a frame and concealed cistern.
  • A wall-hung vanity with drawers.
  • Water-efficient sanitaryware and taps.

Storage

Niches in the shower for bottles, a vanity with drawers under the basin and a cabinet or mirror with storage. A built-in niche is also a typical feature of Mediterranean style.

Heating

An electric or hydronic towel rail adds comfort and helps things dry. If the home has underfloor heating, the bathroom is where it is most appreciated.

The order of work

Strip-out, services, pressure and drain tests, waterproofing, finishes, fitting sanitaryware and final details. Skipping a test or a layer to save time costs dearly later. We go into detail in the order of trades.
