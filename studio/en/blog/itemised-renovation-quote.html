PLANNING THE WORKS

Itemised renovation quote: how to read it

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Two quotes with the same price can describe two different jobs. The difference is in what each line includes. Reading a quote properly is the best protection against the most feared surprise in a renovation: the extra nobody had mentioned.

What an itemised quote is

It is a quote divided into chapters and, within each chapter, line items with a description, unit of measurement, quantity and price. In the wall finishes chapter, for example, you would see something like "bathroom wall tiling with 30 x 60 ceramic tile, including adhesive and grouting, 22 m²". That way you know what is being done, how much and with what.

Usual chapters in a renovation

  • Preparation and demolition: protection, strip-out, rubble removal and skip.
  • Building work: partitions, screeds, builder's work for services.
  • Plumbing and drainage: pipes, drains, sanitaryware, taps.
  • Electrics: fuse board, circuits, switches and sockets, light points, certificate.
  • Heating, cooling and ventilation: equipment, ducts, underfloor heating if any.
  • Interior and exterior joinery: doors, wardrobes, windows.
  • Wall and floor finishes: flooring, tiling, painting.
  • Kitchen and bathrooms: units, worktops, appliances if included.
  • Waste management, final clean and safety.

Five questions for every line

  1. Does the description state the material, its quality or its brand and model?
  2. Does it include materials or labour only?
  3. Is the quantity measured from drawings or estimated?
  4. Is removal of the existing item included?
  5. What happens if something different turns up when it is opened?

When a line just says "bathroom renovation" or "full electrics", ask for a breakdown. It isn't mistrust: it is the only way to compare.

What is usually left out

Check specifically whether these are included:

  • Licences, fees and municipal taxes.
  • Technical project and site supervision when the work requires them.
  • Installation certificates.
  • Skip and permit to occupy the public road.
  • Appliances, light fittings and furniture.
  • Ceiling paint, skirting, finishing details and the final clean.

How to compare quotes

Don't compare totals; compare line by line. Make a table with the chapters as rows and the companies as columns. Big differences in a line almost always hide something: a cheaper material, a smaller quantity or work that hasn't been included. Ask about them.

Be wary of a quote much cheaper than the rest without a clear reason. In renovation, cheap usually arrives later in the form of add-ons.

The contingency

In an older home, opening walls and floors brings surprises: a pipe in poor condition, a beam that needs reinforcing, hidden damp. Set aside 10 to 15 % of the total for those cases. If it isn't used, all the better; if it is needed, the work doesn't stop.

How to avoid extras

The rule is simple: no change is carried out without being priced and approved in writing. If you decide to change a material during the work, or something unexpected appears, you should receive a price first and accept it. That way you always know how much you have spent.

This is one of the jobs of renovation project management: checking real measurements, making sure what is installed matches what was quoted, and warning you before, not after, about any deviation.

Payment terms

The sensible approach is to pay against completed, verifiable milestones, not in advance. A common structure is a deposit at the start, stage payments as phases close (services, finishes, final details) and a final amount after snagging and handover.
