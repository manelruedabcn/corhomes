STYLE AND WELLBEING

Wellbeing at home: light, air, quiet and order

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

We spend much of our lives indoors, which means our home affects how we sleep, rest and feel. A renovation is the best opportunity to improve those conditions, and almost all of them are decided at the design stage, not in the decoration.

Natural light during the day

Daylight helps regulate the sleep-wake rhythm and makes spaces feel alive. In a renovation you can gain it by opening up layouts, enlarging openings, choosing pale finishes or bringing light into interior areas. We develop this in how to gain natural light in a renovation.

Warm artificial light at night

As evening falls, a warmer, lower light is better. Floor and table lamps, indirect strips and dimmers let you move from working light to resting light. In living areas and bedrooms, colour temperatures of 2700 to 3000 K work well. There is more detail in layered lighting.

Clean air

A well-insulated house needs well-designed ventilation. Otherwise humidity, odours and CO2 build up and condensation appears. Opening windows helps, but in winter or summer it means losing temperature. A heat recovery ventilation system renews the air continuously and recovers much of its energy.

Also look after what the house itself gives off: paints, varnishes and adhesives low in volatile organic compounds, certified timber and natural textiles.

A stable temperature

Thermal comfort isn't just air temperature. The temperature of walls and floors, draughts and humidity all play a part. Good insulation, efficient windows and radiant systems such as underfloor heating give a more even and pleasant feeling than a unit blowing hot or cold air. We explain it in heat pumps and underfloor heating.

Quiet

Constant noise is tiring even when we don't notice it. Acoustically insulated windows, insulated partitions, underlay beneath floors and sound-absorbing textiles greatly reduce the sense of noise. We cover it in home acoustics.

Visual order

A house with well-planned storage feels calmer. Built-in wardrobes, bespoke joinery, pantries and entrance areas with room for coats, bags and keys stop things piling up in sight. Order isn't a question of character: often it is a question of everything having its place.

Contact with nature

Natural materials, plants, views outside and textures you can touch make a space feel kinder. You don't need to fill the house with plants; a well-framed window, a cared-for patio or a lime-plastered wall is enough.

Spaces for each moment

Wellbeing also depends on having a place for each thing: somewhere to work that doesn't take over the living room, a corner to read or switch off, a kitchen where you can cook together. We share ideas in calm spaces at home and home office design.

A checklist for the design

  • Does each main room get enough daylight?
  • Can the lighting be dimmed, and is there warm light for the evening?
  • How is the air renewed without opening windows in midwinter or midsummer?
  • Are there draughts or cold walls?
  • Which noises bother you today, and where do they come from?
  • Does everything have a place to be put away?
