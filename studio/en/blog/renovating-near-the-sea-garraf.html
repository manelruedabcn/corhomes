PLANNING THE WORKS

Renovating a home near the sea in Garraf

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Living near the sea in Sitges, Vilanova i la Geltrú, Cubelles or Sant Pere de Ribes has many advantages, and also some demands on the house. Salt air, humidity and strong sun wear materials and services faster than inland. A coastal renovation has to account for this from day one.

Salt air and corrosion

Sea air carries salts that settle on surfaces and speed up corrosion of metals. It affects railings, hardware, screws, window frames, outdoor climate units and exterior lights.

Recommendations:

  • Marine-grade stainless steel, such as AISI 316, outdoors and in exposed areas. Standard stainless steel can stain and rust.
  • Aluminium with marine-grade powder coating, with a quality certification for aggressive atmospheres.
  • Hardware and fixings with the same level of protection as the frames.
  • Climate units with anti-corrosion treatment on the outdoor coil.

Ambient humidity

On the coast, relative humidity is often high, especially in summer. If the house doesn't ventilate well, condensation, mould in wardrobes, cold walls and that closed-up smell appear in second homes that stand empty for a while.

  • Good heat recovery ventilation renews the air even if nobody opens the windows.
  • Climate systems with a dehumidifying function greatly improve summer comfort.
  • On ground floors, check for rising damp and treat it before applying finishes.

Materials that last

  • Lime and mineral paints on façades and interiors: they breathe and hold up well.
  • Natural stone and porcelain outdoors and on terraces, with a non-slip finish.
  • Treated timber or durable species outdoors, with maintenance planned.
  • Quality joints and seals, because they are the first thing to degrade.

The sun

The coastal sun is intense and punishes south and west orientations. Exterior shading, awnings with UV-resistant fabrics and solar-control glass prevent overheating and protect floors and furniture from fading. We go into detail in cooling a Mediterranean home.

Windows and frames

As well as marine-grade coating, windows should be very airtight, because the easterly wind drives water and sand. Good seals, well-resolved sills and careful sealing to the wall. The criteria are in energy-efficient windows.

Terraces and outdoor areas

On the coast, the terrace is part of the home. Non-slip paving with the right fall, drains sized for heavy rain, checked waterproofing and well-designed shade with pergolas, awnings or planting. Continuity between inside and outside is one of the hallmarks of Mediterranean style.

Second homes

If the house isn't used all year, think about its upkeep: automatic ventilation, scheduled dehumidification, closures that protect from the weather and materials that don't demand constant care. And about managing the work remotely: with a single point of contact who keeps you updated, you don't need to be on site every week.

Coastal bylaws

Coastal towns often have specific conditions on façades, colours, windows or visible elements. Check them before designing; there is guidance in renovation permits.
