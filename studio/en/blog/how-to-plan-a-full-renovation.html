PLANNING THE WORKS

How to plan a full home renovation, step by step

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Almost every renovation that goes wrong has one thing in common: it started before it was thought through. A wall came down on Monday and by Tuesday there were three urgent decisions nobody had anticipated. Planning does not delay the work. It is how the work takes as long as it should and costs what was agreed.

1. Define what you want to achieve, not just what you want to change

Before talking about tiles, write down how you want to live in the house once it is finished. "I want to cook with the family without bumping into each other", "I need a quiet place to work", "I want the living room to stay warm in winter". Those sentences are what later decide whether to open up the kitchen, insulate a façade or replace the windows.

Separate the essentials from the nice-to-haves, too. It will help when the budget forces you to choose, and it almost always does.

2. Technical survey and diagnosis

A full renovation starts with knowing what is behind the walls. The survey should check:

  • The state of the electrical installation and the fuse board.
  • Water pipes and drains, especially if they are lead or iron.
  • Damp, cracks and the condition of floors and beams.
  • External windows and doors, and insulation.
  • Which walls are load-bearing and which can be moved.

This diagnosis avoids the phrase that makes a renovation more expensive than any other: "we didn't know about that".

3. Design and decisions

With the diagnosis in hand, the proposal is drawn up: layout, services, light points, sockets, heating and cooling, and finishes. The more you decide on paper, the less gets improvised on site. A change of mind on a drawing costs an eraser; on site it costs days and money.

Spend time on what you won't see but will notice: where the sockets go, how each room is lit, where the ducts run. In our guide to layered lighting we explain why lighting is decided before the walls are closed.

4. An itemised quote

Ask for a broken-down quote: demolition, building work, plumbing, electrics, joinery, finishes, waste management. A fixed price without a breakdown doesn't let you compare or know what is included. We go into detail in how to read an itemised renovation quote.

Always keep a contingency. In an older house, 10 to 15 % of the total is a prudent reference.

5. Permits

Depending on the work, you will need either a prior notification or a building licence from the town hall. It isn't a minor formality: starting without it can end in a fine or a halted site. There is a summary in renovation permits in Catalonia.

6. Work schedule

A good schedule orders the trades so that each one arrives at the right moment and they don't get in each other's way. It also sets milestones you can check: demolition finished, services closed in, wall and floor finishes, final details. In the order of trades in a renovation you will see the usual sequence.

Ask about long-lead items too, such as made-to-measure joinery, stone worktops or kitchens. They are ordered at the start, not when they are needed.

7. Supervision during the work

During the build someone has to look at the site through the eyes of the design: checking that what is installed is what was specified, that deadlines are met and that surprises are decided with information, not in a rush. That role is renovation project management, and it is what turns a plan into a build that looks like the plan.

8. Handover and snagging

Before the work is signed off, there is a room-by-room check: joints, junctions, doors that close properly, sockets that work, taps that don't drip. Whatever is noted at that point is fixed before handover. Ask for the paperwork as well: warranties, equipment manuals and installation certificates.

A checklist to keep handy

  1. Written goals and clear priorities.
  2. A technical diagnosis of the home.
  3. Drawings with layout, lighting, sockets and climate control.
  4. An itemised quote with a contingency.
  5. The right permit before starting.
  6. A schedule with milestones and early orders.
  7. One person accountable for the work.
  8. Final snagging and documentation.
