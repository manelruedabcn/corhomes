SPACES

Home office: light, ergonomics and quiet

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Working from home at the dining table works for a few days. In the long run it tires the body, invades family life and makes it hard to switch off. A well-designed office, even a small one, changes the way you work and the way you rest.

Where to put it

Ideally a room of its own, however small, with a door. If that isn't possible:

  • A corner of the guest bedroom.
  • A space under the stairs or in the loft.
  • Part of the living room separated by a bookcase or a glazed partition.
  • A cupboard turned into a desk that closes at the end of the day.

Keep it away from the kitchen and the noisiest areas of the house.

Natural light, best from the side

A desk placed perpendicular to the window avoids reflections on the screen and glare. With the window behind you, the screen reflects the light; facing it, the light dazzles. A curtain or blind that softens the sun helps in the brightest hours.

Suitable artificial light

Combine good general light with an adjustable task lamp. For working, colour temperatures of 3500 to 4000 K help concentration; if the office shares the room with other uses, dimmable light lets you switch to something warmer when you finish. Look for fittings with good colour rendering. We explain it in layered lighting.

Ergonomics

  • Desk: about 72 to 75 cm high, or adjustable if you want to alternate between sitting and standing.
  • Chair: height-adjustable, with lumbar support and armrests.
  • Screen: top edge at eye level and roughly an arm's length away.
  • Feet: flat on the floor or on a footrest.
  • Space: enough desk depth to set the screen back and move your arms.

Quiet

For calls and concentration, acoustic insulation matters. A solid door with seals, a lining wall if the office borders a noisy area, and textiles that cut echo. There is more detail in home acoustics.

For video calls, a background wall with shelves, books or a soft texture also stops sound bouncing around.

Sockets and connectivity

This is what gets forgotten most in a renovation and noticed most afterwards:

  • Several sockets at desk height, with USB ports.
  • A wired network point for a stable connection.
  • Good Wi-Fi coverage or an access point nearby.
  • Conduits so cables aren't on show.

Storage

Closed cupboards for papers and supplies, shelves for what you use every day and a clear desk surface. The less clutter in sight, the easier it is to concentrate, and to switch off when you finish.

Temperature and air

Spending many hours in a small room demands good ventilation and a stable temperature. Check the window and the heating and cooling in that space; heat recovery ventilation keeps the air fresh with the door closed.

Separating work and life

An office you can close helps end the working day. If the space is shared, a curtain, a sliding door or a cupboard that hides the desk does the same job.
