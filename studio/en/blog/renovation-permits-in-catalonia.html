PLANNING THE WORKS

Permits to renovate your home in Catalonia

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

Before lifting the first tile, it is worth knowing what the town hall requires. In Catalonia, home renovations generally fall under one of two procedures: prior notification (comunicació prèvia) or a building licence (llicència d'obres). Which one applies depends on what you plan to do and on each municipality's bylaws.

Important note

This article is general guidance. Each town hall has its own bylaws and they can change. Before starting, always check with your municipality or on its online portal which procedure applies to your project.

Prior notification

This is a simplified procedure: you notify the town hall that you are going to carry out work, submit the documents requested and, generally, you can start without waiting for a decision. It usually applies to minor technical work, for example:

  • Interior renovations that don't change the layout, the structure or the façade.
  • Replacing finishes, floors, tiling or sanitaryware.
  • Renewing a kitchen or bathroom without changing the layout.
  • Interior painting and repairs.

Building licence

This requires the town hall to review the project and grant permission before work begins. It is usually needed when the work:

  • Significantly changes the interior layout.
  • Affects the structure: load-bearing walls, beams, floor slabs.
  • Changes the façade, openings or exterior appearance.
  • Adds floor area or changes the use.

In these cases it is common to require a project or technical report signed by a qualified professional, and sometimes formal technical supervision during the work.

Other permits people forget

  • Occupying the public road for the rubble skip or scaffolding.
  • Owners' association: if you touch common elements or the façade, or the work affects neighbours, check the building rules and notify the community. Replacing windows, for instance, may be subject to the building's appearance.
  • Heritage protection: in historic centres or listed buildings the conditions are stricter.

Documents usually requested

It varies by town hall, but it is common to provide:

  • A description of the work and its location.
  • The construction budget, used as the basis for the construction tax.
  • Drawings or sketches of the current and proposed state if there are changes.
  • A signed technical project when the work requires one.
  • A construction waste management plan.

Taxes and fees

Along with the permit, you usually pay the construction, installations and works tax (ICIO), calculated on the budget, plus a municipal processing fee. It is worth asking beforehand, because it doesn't always appear in the contractor's quote. We cover it in how to read an itemised quote.

What happens if you don't apply

Work without the right permit can end in a stop order, a fine or the obligation to legalise what was done, with the cost and delay that entails. It can also cause problems when selling the home, or with insurance if something happens.

In Garraf and Penedès

Sitges, Vilanova i la Geltrú, Sant Pere de Ribes, Cubelles, Vilafranca del Penedès and El Vendrell each have their own bylaws and online procedures. Near the sea or in historic centres there may be additional conditions on façades, colours or windows.
