ENERGY AND COMFORT

Cooling your home: options for a Mediterranean climate

Updated 26 November 2026 · 3 min read

On the Mediterranean coast, summers are getting longer and nights warmer. Cooling a house well doesn't start with the air conditioner; it starts with keeping the heat out. What doesn't get in doesn't need to be removed.

First, keep the heat out

Exterior shading

Sun that passes through glass heats the room. It is much more effective to stop it outside, with shutters, awnings, lattices or eaves, than with curtains inside. On west-facing façades, with the low afternoon sun, exterior shading is almost essential.

Insulation and windows

Insulation isn't only for winter: it also slows the heat coming in through walls and roof in summer. Windows with low-emissivity, solar-control glass reduce heat gain without taking away light. We go into detail in energy-efficient windows.

Roofs and top floors

The top floor takes the full sun. Insulating the roof and, where possible, using light-coloured finishes or ventilated roofs makes a big difference.

Ventilate with judgement

Cross ventilation, opening windows on opposite façades, cools the house when the outside air is cooler than inside: at night and early in the morning. During the hot hours, it is better to close up and shade. Ceiling fans, with very low consumption, increase the feeling of coolness and let you raise the air-conditioning thermostat by a couple of degrees.

Cooling systems

Air-to-air heat pump with split units

The most widespread solution: one outdoor unit and indoor units in each room. Quick to install and effective. Its weak points are the look of wall-mounted units and the need for one unit per room.

Ducted systems

A single machine, usually in a suspended ceiling, distributes air through grilles in each room. It is more discreet and spreads air better, but it needs ceiling space and good zoning so you don't cool the whole house at once.

Heat pump with fan coils

If the house has an air-to-water heat pump, fan coils use the system's chilled water to cool and dehumidify. They are a good option when you want a single unit for heating, cooling and hot water.

Cooling floors

Underfloor heating can cool in summer with water at about 14 to 18 °C. It is silent and very comfortable, but its capacity is limited and it doesn't remove humidity. On the coast, where relative humidity is high, it needs dew-point control to avoid condensation and is best complemented with dehumidification. We explain it in heat pumps and underfloor heating.

Humidity counts too

In Garraf and along the Penedès coast, the problem is often not just temperature but humidity. With high humidity, 26 °C feels much heavier. A system that dehumidifies, even if it doesn't lower the temperature as much, can give more comfort. Mechanical ventilation with air treatment also helps control it.

Size it properly

An oversized unit cools quickly but switches on and off constantly, dehumidifies worse and uses more energy. An undersized one can't cope on hot days. The calculation should account for orientation, glazed area, insulation and the use of each room.

Where to put the units

Outdoor units need airflow and access, and they make noise. In apartment buildings, the community and local bylaws may limit where they go. Near the sea, choose units with anti-corrosion treatment; we cover it in renovating near the sea.

A logical order

  1. Shade from the sun on the outside.
  2. Improve insulation and windows.
  3. Use night ventilation and fans.
  4. Choose the system that fits the renovation and local humidity.
  5. Size it by calculation, not by eye.
